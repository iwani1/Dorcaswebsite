Why Adinkra

A piece that says something

Every symbol we use already meant something long before we made jewellery from it. We did not invent these meanings, and we do not need to — we just make them wearable.

Sankofa Go back and fetch it The bird flies forward with its head turned back. It is not wrong to return for what you left behind. Gye Nyame Except for God The most widely worn of all Adinkra symbols — everything that has happened and will happen is already known. Akoma The heart Patience, tolerance and endurance — a promise that the heart can hold more than the moment it is in. Duafe The wooden comb Given as a token of love and care: beauty is something tended, not something owned. Dwennimmen The ram's horns The ram fights with its horns, and the same horns mean humility. Strength, and the wisdom to keep it quiet. Adinkrahene Chief of all symbols Three concentric circles, and the king of the Adinkra set — the standard the others are measured against.
